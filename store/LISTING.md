## Name
Dicey Depths

## Category
Games

## Tagline (one line)
Roll your dice face up, then decide how to spend them.

## Description

You are a walking six-sided die on a game show.

Every turn you roll your dice face up, and then decide how to spend them across the
equipment you brought in. You always see the randomness BEFORE you commit, so a bad roll
is a puzzle to solve rather than a punishment to absorb.

Drag a die onto a card, tap one then the other, or play entirely with the buttons.

Each card takes a die that matches its slot — any, odd, even, 4+, 3- or an exact number.
A card fires once per turn, so a spare die is worth nothing without a card to put it in.
Fussy cards pay better: one that only accepts a 1 hits far harder than one that takes
anything, and is dead weight on the turn you don't roll one.

Status effects attack your rolls, not your health. Frozen flattens your best die. Burn
sets one alight and charges you 2 HP to put it out. Shocked takes a card offline until
you spend a die clearing it. Every debuff is another puzzle.

TWO CLASSES
The Warrior rolls dice and rerolls them. The Robot has no dice at all — it builds each
number on a CPU meter, pushing for more and losing the lot if it goes past six.

FIVE EPISODES
Each is a ruleset that rewrites the game rather than a difficulty step, and each ends in
its own boss. Start with upgraded weapons and take a curse every turn. Watch every status
effect change meaning. Lose a piece of equipment after every fight. Clear all five to
unlock three Bonus Rounds.

ENDLESS
No boss, no ceiling. Enemies get tougher with depth, and past floor four each floor throws
one of the campaign rulesets at you. Your ten best runs are kept on the watch.

Three difficulties. Easy is very hard to lose. Hard adds a named curse to every floor.
Lose a fight and the run is over.

Built for the Pebble Time 2 and Pebble Round 2, with full touch support and a layout that
follows the curve of the round display. No phone required — everything runs on the watch.

Inspired by Dicey Dungeons by Terry Cavanagh.
